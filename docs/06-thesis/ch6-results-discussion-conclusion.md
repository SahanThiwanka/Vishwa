## 6.3 Discussion

### 6.3.1 What the contamination finding means

The finding in §5.3 has consequences beyond this study.

The SBA National dataset is widely used in credit-scoring research and teaching. A
model given `Term` and free to split on its exact value inherits an artefact worth
up to 0.34 AUC. The roundness probe — a single boolean with no economic content —
reaches 0.887. Any published result on this dataset in that region, obtained from
a tree ensemble with `Term` included, is likely to be measuring the artefact
rather than credit risk.

The general lesson is about **how anomalies are treated**. The finding emerged
only because an AUC of 0.97 was treated as a symptom rather than a success. Had
it been reported as a result — which it easily could have been, since it exceeded
every published benchmark — this thesis would contain a headline finding that was
entirely an artefact. The methodological rule in §3.6.3 is the transferable part.

### 6.3.2 What the negative result means

The expert scorecard failed to discriminate on SBA-observable proxies (AUC
0.41–0.53). Two explanations remain live and the evidence does not separate them:
the proxies are inadequate, or the a priori bands are wrong.

Either way the methodological conclusion holds, and it is the useful one:
**substituting available proxies for the intended criteria tests the proxies, not
the model.** This is why elicitation is a methodological requirement here rather
than a fallback for missing data.

There is a broader point for practice. Institutions without clean historical
default data cannot fit a scorecard, and this study shows they cannot borrow
someone else's dataset to validate one either. Expert elicitation is not a
second-best option in that situation; it is the only sound one.

### 6.3.3 On temporal validation

Under the clean specification, logistic regression scored 0.4565 on temporal
validation — **below chance**. Trained on 1990–2003 (9.1% default) and tested on
2004–2010 (35.9% default), the model did not merely lose accuracy; its ranking
inverted.

For practice, this is the most immediately actionable result in the thesis. A
linear scorecard fitted during benign conditions can rank borrowers *backwards*
under stress. Given Sri Lanka's recent macroeconomic volatility, a scorecard
calibrated on pre-crisis SME lending should not be assumed to hold, and temporal
rather than random validation should be the default.

### 6.3.4 On the system

The system demonstrates that a narrative appraisal instrument can be formalised
without discarding the judgement it encodes — 28 criteria computed from figures,
21 captured as structured linguistic judgements, every one traceable to a clause.

Two design decisions proved more consequential than expected.

The **completeness gate** (§4.5) arose from a real defect found in testing: a
14%-complete file produced a confident recommendation. The gate is a refusal to
answer rather than a score adjustment, because a discounted score would have
preserved the false impression that the system had an opinion.

**Storing the full result document and model version** with each appraisal means
recommendations remain reproducible after the model changes. Since the placeholder
weights are due to be replaced, an appraisal signed under one model must still be
explainable under it later.

## 6.4 Limitations

**Empirical validation is on a proxy dataset from another jurisdiction.** SBA data
reflects U.S. government-guaranteed lending. Nothing transfers directly to Sri
Lankan SME credit.

**The criteria tree is not empirically validated.** No dataset contains its
variables. Chapter 5 validates scoring machinery, not criteria.

**The fuzzy layer is not exercised by the validation.** All SBA-observable
criteria are quantitative and enter as degenerate triangular fuzzy numbers, under
which the fuzzy weighted average reduces exactly to a weighted arithmetic mean. On
that dataset the fuzzy machinery does no work.

**The development objective is barely observable.** Only job creation and
retention proxy it; five of the nine items in clause 5 have no counterpart in the
data. It is a design contribution, not an empirically validated one.

**Elicitation is small-n and purposive.** Weights represent the respondents'
judgement, not Sri Lankan credit practice.

**No inter-rater reliability study was conducted.** This is the most significant
omission. The research is premised on manual appraisal being inconsistent, and
that premise is argued rather than measured.

**No field evaluation.** No officer has used the system on live applications; there
is no usability or acceptance evidence.

**Novelty of the contamination finding is not established as priority.** A
literature search found no prior report, but the search was not exhaustive.

## 6.5 Future work

**Measure the inconsistency.** Have N officers appraise the same M files
independently and compute inter-rater reliability, then repeat with the system.
This directly tests the premise of the research and would be the strongest
possible addition. It requires only practitioner time and constructed cases.

**Establish the contamination mechanism.** Determining why `Term` behaves as it
does — through SBA data documentation or the agency itself — would convert a
caution into a full account, and is publishable independently.

**Validate on institutional data.** With permission and anonymisation, even a few
hundred historical files with outcomes would allow the criteria tree itself to be
tested rather than a proxy.

**Field evaluation.** Deploy alongside the existing process and compare turnaround
time, consistency and officer acceptance.

## 6.6 Conclusion

This study set out to formalise a Sri Lankan state bank's narrative SME appraisal
instrument into an explainable, dual-objective decision-support system.

The artefact was built. A 49-criterion model was derived clause by clause from the
People's Bank appraisal form, implemented in a working system that scores credit
risk and development impact separately, decomposes every score into per-criterion
contributions, withholds a recommendation when too little has been assessed, and
exports its output in the bank's own report format.

The empirical work produced results the study did not set out to find. The most
substantial contribution is not the model but a caution about the data others use
to evaluate such models: the `Term` field in the widely used SBA National dataset
carries outcome information, inflating gradient-boosting performance by up to 0.34
AUC. Alongside it sits a negative result — an expert scorecard that failed to
discriminate on proxy variables — from which follows the methodological point that
instrument-specific appraisal models cannot be validated on datasets lacking their
variables.

RQ1 is answered: the formalisation method is demonstrated and the artefact exists.
RQ3 is answered narrowly and mostly in the negative, with the limits stated. RQ2
and RQ4 depend on elicitation data and are reported according to what was
obtained.

The claim this thesis does **not** make is that a better credit scoring model has
been built. The evidence does not support that, and Chapter 2 establishes that the
methods used are not themselves new. What it offers instead is a transferable
method for formalising institutional appraisal instruments, a working system that
implements it, and two findings about evaluation that should change how others
approach the same problem.
